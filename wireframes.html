04A — Signal Checklist — Before Reveal
The Signal
← Problem Selection

Decision signal

Could a rules engine solve this?

Is the problem fundamentally ambiguous, or just complex?

Why it matters

AI adds cost, latency, and unpredictability. If a rules engine handles the job, AI is a worse solution dressed up as a better one.

The decision rules can be enumerated explicitly
Inputs are structured (not free text, images, audio, or video)
The right answer is the same regardless of context
Edge cases are rare or known in advance
You can describe the rules without using "similar," "probably," or "feels like"
Score: 2 / 5
04B — Signal Checklist — After Reveal

Signal

Could a rules engine solve this?

Rules can be enumerated explicitly
Inputs are structured
Same answer regardless of context
Edge cases are rare or known
No "similar" / "probably" in rules
2 / 5

Guidance

Leans toward AI

The pattern-matching across messy inputs likely justifies the complexity. AI earns its place when the rules cannot be enumerated and the cost of getting it wrong is bounded.

Evidence

"That's the error I keep seeing. Teams get excited about deploying AI and skip the fundamental analysis of whether AI actually solves their problem better than what they already have."

Jim Iyoob, CRO at Etech Global Services Quote ETS Labs ↗

A team spent six months deploying ML for call routing. Their existing rules-based system ran at 99.8% accuracy. The ML model achieved 87% — adding latency, consuming more compute, and producing unexplainable failures.

Failure mode High confidence

Rules-based systems deploy in 6–8 weeks. AI systems require 12–16 weeks, including data preparation, model training, and validation.

Act 03 — Consequences

You chose

Build a RAG pipeline

You shipped RAG in 11 days. Retrieval accuracy jumped from 61% to 79%. Churn slowed. Three months later the team identified the 20% of queries that still underperformed — and had clean data to fine-tune on those cases specifically.

RAG was the expert choice — ship fast, measure, then fine-tune only the underperforming slice.
